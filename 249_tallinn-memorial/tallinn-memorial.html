Maarjamäe Memorial

Tallinn, Estonia

Overlooking Tallinn Bay, Maria's Hill is a sprawling memorial park defined by an unmistakably Soviet vibe to it. Its large open spaces and relatively narrow pathways feel like they are designed to make visitors seem small. The 35-metre obelisk casts a long shadow across the landscape, next to an extinguished eternal flame which once warmed the oversized sculpted hands of the worker. Throughout, brutalist concrete shapes protrude from the ground as if refusing to stay buried.

Built to commemorate the Bolsheviks who died during the 1918 Russian Civil War, this park carries complex historical weight, since several of those honored by this memorial fought against Allied forces and against Estonian independence. It's been a tension point for the city since the withdrawal of the Soviets in 1991 and, especially, since the start of the Russia-Ukraine war.

Since Estonia regained its independence from Soviet occupation, the future of this memorial has been in question. While the grass is still cut and graffiti kept away, it's clear that repairs are not a priority for the city. Broken tiles and temporary fences have littered this site for years.

The remaining mixed messages of this Soviet-era memorial are counterbalanced by the more modern memorial just on the other side of the park, which commemorates the over 75,000 people who from 1940 to 1991 were either murdered, deported, or imprisoned during the Soviet occupation of Estonia. A cause which is much more aligned with the values of modern Estonia.
