Linnahall Theater

Tallinn, Estonia

I first visited this semi-abandoned cultural building a few years ago, but at that time only from the outside. On this visit to Estonia, I got a chance to see a lot of the inside, via a tour of the concert hall and with a bit of luck, also the ice rink. But the ice rink is a story for another day.

Originally named the V. I. Lenin Palace of Culture and Sport, this long and flat building in the centre of Tallinn was constructed as a satellite venue for the 1980 Moscow Summer Olympics. The Russian influence on this Estonian Soviet-era architecture can be clearly seen in the building's brutalist design, but also in how the letter 'N' has been installed incorrectly in the entrance lobby for decades, as shown above.

On this visit, I went inside to discover a few of the theatre's wonders: from the tone-on-tone dark green walls and fittings of the coat check, broken by the warmth of the wooden tabletops, to the dated electronics and patchy soundproofing backstage, and finally, the beautiful 4,200-seater concert hall, featured in the famous Tenet scene, which is luckily decaying at a slightly slower rate than the exterior of the building.
