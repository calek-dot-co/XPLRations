We Are Mountains

Stepanakert / Xankəndi, Azerbaijan

Armenia and Azerbaijan. Opposites in religion, world-view, and vision of the future. Yet they've been sharing a small part of the Caucasus for centuries. There is a lot to say about their conflict, but it'll take at least another visit to each country for me to be ready to start to unpack it. Until then, a visit to a cultural monument that has literally been at the centre of their land war would have to do.

Built from volcanic tuff by Sargis Baghdasaryan in 1967, the monument depicts the locals' vision of Grandmother and Grandfather. It is also known as "Tatik-Papik" (տատիկ-պապիկ) in the Armenian dialect, or "Dedo-Babo" (Դեդո-Բաբո) in the Karabakh dialect. While the monument is widely regarded as a symbol of the Armenian heritage of the Nagorno-Karabakh region, since the 2023 Armenian-Azerbaijani war, it is currently in Azerbaijani territory.

While June 2026 reports of the monument's destruction appear to be AI-fuelled disinformation, it's not hard to imagine vandalism and neglect of the monument eventually leading to a similar outcome.
