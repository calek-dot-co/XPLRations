BS-25 Fire Correction Tower

Somewhere, Poland

This coastal defence tower is like many others I've visited along the Baltic Sea coast. Built to house fire correction equipment, it once guided nearby guns that fired into the Gulf of Gdansk. It was a core part of local defence for decades, then it was abandoned.

But unlike the many other towers I'm visited, this one isn't hidden in the middle of a coastal forest. In the years since it was abandoned, modern life and industry have grown up around this tower.

Accessing the tower was easy thanks to public roads that led into the city’s port infrastructure. You could simply park along the shoulder and walk onto a narrow strip of unclaimed land that housed a small hill and the tower, situated between an oil terminal and a coal terminal.

With the bunker doors at the foot of the hill well secured, only the small open window at the base of the tower provided access. Positioned at roughly head height from the grass outside, this opening leads directly to the midpoint of an internal staircase, demanding a careful entry to avoid a fall into the basement.

The tower's interior has seen better days. Stripped of all equipment, covered in coal dust, and with every inch of the floor sprayed with pigeon poop, I was very glad to have remembered my gloves for this trip.

As I stood at the top of the tower, took in the view, and took the occasional photo, I noticed movement to my left. It was a gas terminal security guard, who either from my flashlight or silhouette noticed that I was in the tower. After a quick word into his radio, I could see another guard approaching from another sector.

Not wanting to get caught up in trouble, or be mistaken for someone with an ulterior motive in presently alert Poland, I quickly left the tower and returned to my car while the security guards made another phone call. And I drove off not a moment too soon, because as I pulled out of the port, a security car crossed my path, lights flashing and racing towards the tower. Whether by luck or something else, they didn't seem to know it was me they were looking for.
