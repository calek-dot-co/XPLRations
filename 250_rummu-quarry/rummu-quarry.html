Flooded Quarry

Rummu, Estonia

In the countryside of Estonia, a former gravel quarry, now flooded by groundwater, sits on the edge of the small rural town of Rummu. The quarry-turned-lake is closely linked to its two neighbours: first, a 48-metre quarry slag heap, and secondly, the former Rummu prison.

The quarry started operating in the 1930s as a source of limestone and Vasalemma marble. From the Soviet occupation in June 1940 until the 1990s, excavation was performed by Murru and Rummu prisoners serving their time with hard labour. After Estonia regained its independence, multiple prisons merged into this site before they all eventually closed in 2012. The closure of the prisons brought the mining and pumps to a standstill, resulting in groundwater quickly flooding everything, including buildings and a mining excavator, which still remain there today.

I first visited a few years ago in autumn and took these photos when the water, leaves, and gravel all glowed in the autumn sunlight. From the entrance, a short hike takes you to the top of the slag heap, past rain-eroded gravel spines, and to views of the lake and sunken buildings. Rummu quarry is well worth a visit on a quiet day.
