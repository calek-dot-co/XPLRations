BS-27 Fire Correction Tower

Hel Peninsula, Poland

On the tip of the Hel peninsula, facing inwards towards the Bay of Gdańsk, is one of two Bateria Artylerii Stałej stations placed in close proximity. Each battery had four guns, support buildings, and a strange-looking tower often referred to by soldiers as the “Kurza Stopka” (Chicken's Foot) due to its weird shape.

This tower, pointing southwards, contains a bunker door, a slender two-level staircase, and two oversized upper floors which each contained rangefinder telescopes that saw far out into the water. They were used to observe enemy ships and artillery accuracy in the hope of connecting the two for lethal effect.

Unfortunately, this tower was the only tower of this type that I visited that was completely locked, so I didn't manage to get any interior photos. But if you're interested in seeing a technical drawing of the tower's historical setup, here is an illustration by the Polish artist A. Wozniakowski (page 10).

Observation Tower 208

Maybe technically part of the nearby BAS-13, this smaller tower sits physically closer to BAS-27, so I'll include it on this page. Placed on a small sand hill, this tower was apparently once fitted with a DM-3 optic rangefinder and supported the other tower above as a Reserve Fire Control Tower.

Costal Lookout

This presumably more modern lookout sits much closer to the beach and provides fantastic views if you are brave enough to climb to the top using the half-broken ladder that sways while you climb.
