BS-13 Fire Correction Tower

Hel Peninsula, Poland

Right next to BAS-27 in the forest of the Hel Peninsula, you can find BAS-13. This 13th coastal defence station fired out into the Baltic Sea, while the 27th fired inwards towards the Gulf of Gdańsk. Together, they protected this coastal area from the 1950s until the last artillery round was fired in 1977. They then slowly decayed until 1993, when the cannons and towers were finally scrapped and abandoned.

BAS is short for ‘Bateria Artylerii Stałej’ in Polish. Similar but a little different to the Russian abreviation BS that I use on this website, which is short for ‘Beregovaia Storona’ (Береговая сторона).

This concrete tower was the Załp-B radar station for this constal battery, so its layout, while similar, is a little bit different from the other fire correction towers I've visited. A smaller building (see below) just east of this main tower was the more traditional fire correction tower which used visual line of sight to guide artillery. Both towers supported four firing positions with 130mm B-13 guns, which in turn were supported by an headquarters bunker, a backup fire correction bunker, an ammunition magazine bunker, two crew shelter bunkers, and a power plant bunker.

Observation Tower 207

A short twentyfive meters away in the woods is this smaller concrete building. It apparently featured a four meter base stereoscopic rangefinder with GPKO-type optics. I have no idea what that means, but a lot of the metal structure remains today, so it was still possible to get a sense of how the whole thing worked. This is assuming you can navigate the missing staircase that was partially replaced by tree branches.
