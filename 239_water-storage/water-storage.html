Underground Water Reservoir

Somewhere, Georgia

If at first you don't succeed, try, try again. It's a phrase that connects ambition, failure, success, and perfection. Yet the path connecting these milestones is rarely straight.

When I first attempted to visit this reservoir a few years ago, it was inaccessible. Steel rebar had been crudely bent and welded over the hatches blocking entry, leaving me with nothing but a view of a ladder disappearing into the darkness below. While it felt a bit like a failure at the time, it was on my list to try again next time I was in Georgia.

But the next visit didn't quite go to plan. There was bad weather, a bite from a street dog, and a Georgia that felt like it was slowly decaying. At first, I wasn't even going to try again, until I was compelled to by a photograph of this reservoir I saw at the local photography exhibition. Two blades of sunlight pierced the underground darkness, revealing the underground concrete cavern.

Luckily, I ended up trying again. On this second attempt, the reservoir was accessible, as a previous visitor had cut and bent the rebar to create a small gap to squeeze through. Down the ladder I went and into another world. It was quite dark, since the cloudy weather blocked the iconic sun rays. But it was a success nonetheless, as you can see by the photos on this page.

But while I had successfully visited, perfection wasn't achieved. So I decided to try for a third time a few days later. It was a bright, sunny morning, ideal for the perfect photo. However, during my wait for good weather, the local police must have cottoned on to the increased activity in this area.

As I started to approach the land the reservoir is on, a tiny white hatchback pulled up and four very large undercover cops piled out. They proceeded to ask for my ID, search my belongings, and question me about my plans. Luckily for me, I wasn't already inside the tanks, so I was still just a tourist. Unluckily for me, the place was now well-watched, and further run-ins with the Georgian police would be best avoided.

Maybe next time, I'll try, try again.
